Can people find Caravancare Cheshire online?

We looked at your website the way Google and a first-time visitor do. Here is what we found, in plain English, with no jargon.

4 of 10

Your site passes 4 of the 10 basic checks. Your site is quick and set up for phones, but six gaps decide whether people find you and what they see when they do. The biggest is your web address itself.

The one that matters most

Typing your web address without the "www" shows an error page.

If someone types caravancarecheshire.co.uk into their phone, the way most people write a web address, they get a page headed "DNS resolution error" instead of your website. Only the version that starts with www works.

Customers often see your address on a card, a van or a recommendation from a friend at the caravan club and type it in themselves. When it fails, many people will not try again with www; they will search instead, and may ring someone else.

This is most likely a setting with the company that holds your web address, and it is usually a quick fix. A new site would make sure every version of your address lands on your home page.

Five smaller things to fix

All five are quick to put right.

To fix

Google is left to write your search listing

Under the blue link in a search result there are two lines describing the business. None of your pages supplies them, so Google picks words from the page itself.

A new site would write them: mobile caravan and motorhome servicing and repairs across Cheshire, at your home or storage site.

To fix

Shared links show no picture

When a customer shares your site, or passes it round a caravan club group, no picture or description goes with the link.

A new site would attach a photograph of you at work and one clear line to every share.

To fix

Your pages have no main heading

Every page should have one main heading that says what it is. It is the first thing a search engine reads. None of your three pages has one.

A new site would open with a heading that says it plainly: mobile caravan servicing across Cheshire, and we come to you.

To fix

Your pictures are labelled with file names

Pictures can carry a short written description, used by image searches and read aloud to people who cannot see the screen. Yours are labelled with file names such as "IMG_3039.jpeg" and "Thetford%20Logo[1]". There are also no photographs of your work on the site, only your name and partner logos.

A new site would describe every picture in plain words, and show real caravans and the work you do on them.

To fix

Google is not handed your details

Websites can give Google a small fact sheet: what the business does, the area it covers and the number to ring. Yours tells Google only that the site is called "Caravancare".

A new site would include that fact sheet, with your phone number, the area you cover and your services, so your number shows up reliably when someone searches for you.

Can AI assistants find you?

More people now ask ChatGPT, Claude or Google's AI answers instead of searching. These tools read your website too. We checked four things they rely on. Your site passes 3 of 4.

Working

AI assistants are allowed in

Nothing on your site turns them away.

Working

The words can be read without running the page

Your words are there in the page itself, so AI tools can read them. There are just very few of them: about 120 on your home page.

To fix

Your phone number and address are written out

Your mobile number sits behind the Book Service and Call buttons but is never written out as text, and there is no address or list of the towns you cover anywhere on the site.

Working

A summary file for AI tools

Your site already has an AI summary file, made automatically by Wix. It describes the business clearly, as a family-run mobile caravan and motorhome service across Cheshire.

Writing your number and the towns you cover out in words is the single most useful change here.

Nobody can make an AI recommend a business, and anyone who offers to is selling nothing. What a website can do is let these tools reach it, read it and get your facts right.

What we would do
